# Svarah Phase 3 summary (error analysis; recommendations only)

Baseline unchanged: `results/svarah_wer.json`, WER 23.07% [17.9-29.5], 1,630 reference words, S 134 / D 44 / I 198. Only the Parakeet TDT 0.6B v3 q8_0 model was used. No benchmark rerun, no production/VAD/ws_bench change, no fix implemented. One extra offline full-clip decode by the same engine (`svarah_offline_decode.py`) supplies audio-path evidence. **No human listened to any audio**, so conclusions that need listening are marked.

Details: `SVARAH_INSERTION_ANALYSIS.md/.json`, `SVARAH_WER_NORMALIZATION_COMPARISON.md`, `SVARAH_LATENCY_MATCHING_AUDIT.md`. Code: `svarah_error_analysis.py`, `make_svarah_md.py`.

## Answers

1. **Largest share of insertions: numeric representation, 84 of 198 (42%).** References hold digit strings (phone, UAN, PRAN, UPI); Parakeet emits one spoken digit word per digit. 26 are pure formatting (equal after policy C); 58 are in digit groups that still differ after C (formatting plus a digit error, not separable without listening). Next: fillers 55 (28%), possible VAD/segmentation 17 (9%), repeated words 6, compound 2, other 34 (17%, mostly words the offline decode also produces). Top 5 clips hold 73 insertions (37%); top 10 hold 116 (59%).
2. **Formatting-related errors: 107 of 376 (28%)** (A to D: number format 95, compound 12). Approximate; digit errors partly hidden when a digit string collapses to one substitution. A further 59 errors (16%) vanish if fillers are ignored; that is a reference-convention question, not counted as formatting.
3. **Genuine recognition errors: not measurable here.** 210 errors (56%) remain after all three adjustments, as an upper bound for genuine differences; they also include unnormalised reference conventions (Rs./rupees, names, spellings). Category "genuine additional words" has 0 assigned tokens because it needs human listening.
4. **Segmentation association: yes, in the relay path.** Whole-clip offline decode scores S 104 / D 14 / I 167 (WER 17.48%) against relay S 134 / D 44 / I 198: the VAD path adds 31 insertions and 30 deletions. Six clips with a wrong final count hold 24 of 44 deletions (55%); in 5 of them the last expected utterance produced no final and its words are in the offline decode; one clip has a duplicate final. 17 inserted tokens sit at final boundaries and are absent offline. Cause of the missing finals is not instrumented (empty-decode drop is consistent, unverified).
5. **Corrected latency (timestamp matching, 6 clips affected):** TTFW n=195, p50 0.889 s, p95 2.174 s, max 7.585 s (old: n=196, 0.891 / 2.172 / 7.585). Final minus utterance end: p50 0.911, p95 1.500, max 1.890 s (old 0.909 / 1.500 / 1.890). Final minus expected flush time: p50 0.413, p95 1.036, max 1.563 s. Aggregate unchanged; per clip, one impossible -3.807 s latency (bengali_01831) is removed, 5 unmatched expected utterances and 1 extra final are reported. Max TTFW 7.585 s is a final-without-partial group in a matched clip.
6. **Address first (proposals, not implemented):** (a) report WER with a declared, separate number-format policy alongside raw, and write the policy down; (b) investigate the missing/duplicate finals at the end of forced-cap clips: instrument why a flush yields no final (largest evidenced loss, 24 deletions); (c) fix timestamp matching in the benchmark client for future runs; (d) decide whether spoken digit output is wanted for the product, since it drives the biggest insertion share; (e) handle fillers by policy once reference convention is confirmed. Each needs your approval.
7. **Is 60 clips enough to justify a production change? No.** One run, 60 clips, one speaker each, groups of 1-4, wide CI (17.9-29.5), concentration in a few clips (top 10 = 59% of insertions), no human-verified categories, and VAD timing is circular. It justifies the investigations in 6(b) and 6(c), not a VAD or model change. A production change needs a repeat run, human-checked sample of the missing-final clips, and ideally a larger set.

## Limitations
- Rule-based categories are heuristics with evidence stored per token; 'other' (34) is a remainder.
- Normalised WER is diagnostic, never an accuracy claim.
- Offline decode is the same model: agreement shows stability, not correctness.
- Partial texts and per-message times were not stored in the baseline run, so partial-level analysis is not possible from it.
